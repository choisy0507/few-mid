01   /   LEARN THE LANGUAGE

How scent connects.

One fragrance can be described in different ways. Begin with three kinds of information.

01 / TYPE OF INFORMATION

Family

The overall direction.

EXAMPLE

Floral

A fragrance family groups scents by their broad character.

02 / TYPE OF INFORMATION

Note

A recognizable scent impression.

EXAMPLE

Rose

A note evokes a specific scent, but does not always mean the ingredient is present.

03 / TYPE OF INFORMATION

Descriptor

How it may feel.

EXAMPLE

Powdery

A descriptor expresses a quality or sensation that can appear across different scents.

EXAMPLE

Floral→Rose→Powdery

03   /   LOOK A LITTLE CLOSER

Powdery and Soapy

Two descriptions that can both suggest cleanliness.

01 / A SENSORY QUALITY

Powdery

TEXTURE / SOFT · DRY · FINE

Think of a soft veil of fine powder, or the delicate dry impression sometimes associated with iris.

Notice the texture, rather than a specific product.

≠
02 / A RECOGNIZABLE ASSOCIATION

Soapy

ASSOCIATION / CLEAN · SUDSY · FRESH

Think of the familiar impression of soap, fresh laundry, or a recently washed surface.

Notice the association, not necessarily a powder-like feeling.

Both may suggest cleanliness, and they can overlap in a fragrance. They do not describe the same thing.

04   /   ABOUT

About the classification.

This project separates fragrance families, notes, and descriptors to make unfamiliar scent language easier to explore. A single term can connect to several families; the context matters.

This is a selected set of examples, not a complete fragrance catalog. Connections describe common associations, not measured similarity.

Family framework: The Perfume Society ↗. Classification and individual scent impressions may differ across sources and people.
